# 0044: The glossary at the side, the sources closed, and the dots under the pointer

- Date: 15 October 2026
- Status: accepted
- Text confirmed by the owner: not yet. An independent agent checks it (working rule 35).
- Diagram: none
- Changes: decision 0005 (each fact shows a light dotted underline), for a screen with a pointer. Decision 0043, item 2 (the glossary was a closed section in the primer).

## Question

The owner did not like three things: whole paragraphs that look underlined, the heading "Words on this tab", and the label "Sources of the top of this tab" with its open list. What changes?

## Decision

1. On a screen with a pointer, the dotted underline of a fact shows only while the pointer is on that fact. A touch screen keeps the underline of decision 0005. The help cursor still shows that a sentence has a preview card.
2. The glossary of each tab is a panel at the right side. A button "Glossary" stays fixed at the right edge of the screen, and on a phone at the bottom right. The button opens and closes the panel, and Esc closes it. A link to `#top-words` opens it.
3. The heading of the glossary is "Glossary".
4. Each list of sources is a closed section with its count, for example "Sources of part 1.3 (24)". "Sources of the top" becomes "Sources of the introduction".

## Reason

The owner said that most paragraphs read as one long underline. The real cause is text that is a chain of sourced facts, and the owner agreed. A rewrite as teaching text is not part of this decision. The owner wants the glossary at hand while scrolling, and the sources out of the way until a reader wants them.

## How we decided

The assistant recommended dots only while the pointer is over a paragraph. The owner said: "for now just fix the underline issue by show the dots only while the pointer is over a paragraph." Then the owner said that a paragraph still underlines all its sentences at once, and agreed to "only the sentence under the pointer". The owner then wrote: "I don't like the heading 'Words on this tab' Also I think this glossary should move to the side where people can expand it anytime as they are scrolling through the screen and moves with the screen. This line sounds weird 'Sources of the top of this tab' and also all of those sources should be in an expandable tab as well." The assistant proposed the panel, the name "Glossary" and the closed lists, and said that the page has only about 100 pixels free at each side at 1280 pixels, so the panel cannot stay open beside the text. The owner said: "YES".
