# C2: a skimmer reads both tabs

One agent saw only the headings, the figure labels and the captions of both tabs (`tools/extract-text.js`, the `.skim.md` files). It wrote one takeaway for each part (`C2-takeaways.md`).

## Result

- Tab 1 works for a skimmer. Each takeaway heading is a claim, and most figures say it again. Part 4.3 is the exception: two panel captions say that four current models reject the settings any and tool, so a skimmer reads that two of the three settings are broken. The point of the part, form against values, is only in the last row of labels.
- Tab 2 works for steps 0 to 8. A skimmer learns the story: code copies the guide and plans 60 slots, one Claude call writes each question, code and two calls check it, a failed try goes back at most 3 times, the owner decides, a reader marks, and code builds the files only after the owner accepts. The numbers land too.
- Tab 2 is weaker on its title promise, "the same principles they test". In the skim, only Fig. 1-2 ties a step to a task statement. The four parts of the build in Claude Code have only headings, and no group heading says why they come after step 8. The labels A1 to A12, B1 to B9 and D1 to D4 are not explained in the skim.

## Headings and figures that seem to disagree (for the owner)

1. 4.3: see above.
2. 2.2: the caption says "four categories", and the figure has five panels ("No matches" is the extra one, the success case).
3. 2.5: the heading names Glob and Edit, and the figure shows only Grep and Read.
4. 3.2: the heading is about sharing skills, and the figure is only about `context: fork`.
5. 3.4: the caption says that Claude "does not edit the source" in plan mode, and a label says "edits wait for approval (usually)".
6. Fig. 1.1-2: three of the four panel captions are written as instructions ("Parse natural language signals ..."), and no panel is marked wrong in the skim. The figure shows them as wrong by color and chips, which the skim does not show.
7. Tab 2, step 1: the sub-heading "An agent decides the next step" stands under "Code plans all 60 questions". It is the other side of a comparison.
8. Tab 2, Fig. 4-2: the caption counts 148 failed checks, and the bars add up to 156 findings. Not an error: one check can have more than one finding. A short note would help.
9. Tab 2, Fig. 5-3 (the design review) stands in the part of the loop of a slot.
10. Tab 2, step 7: "The owner filled the 20 marks of round 1 without reading the questions" alarms a skimmer.

"How we broke it down" (tab 1), "The 9 steps" (tab 2), and the build in Claude Code: 3.1, 1.7 and 3.4. Each needs one caption or one heading that states its point.
