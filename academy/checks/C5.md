# C5: a sceptic reads tab 2

One agent played an experienced engineer who builds with Claude and doubts that mock exams written by a model are good. It read all parts of tab 2 and the cards of the top, steps 1, 4 to 8 and the build in Claude Code, 4.6.

## Verdict

The process is well controlled, and the tab shows its weak points better than most pages of this kind. It does not convince that the questions are good: almost all its evidence is about the process, and little is about the quality of the questions. The title promise holds for steps 1 to 5, partly for step 6, and only as a label for steps 0 and 7.

## Most convincing

Step 1 (ideas with two or more slots fell from 12 to 0), step 8 (300 of 300 fingerprints), step 3 (the longest option, 37 of 52 in mock 1, then near chance), step 5 (M5-S38 traced through the code and fixed), step 4 (the cost with and without the cache).

## Most doubted

1. Call 4a agreed with the answer key 477 times in 477. Either each key is right, or the check cannot find a wrong key. No test gives call 4a a wrong key on purpose. (The page names the model of the build, Claude Opus 5.5, for all calls.)
2. Rule B9: M5-S38 failed B9 four times, then try 5 wrote the old problem again and passed. M5-S33, a copy of a question of mock 2, passed B9 too. The records are on the page, but the page does not draw the conclusion that B9 is not reliable.
3. Step 7: for mocks 4 and 5 the reader was a Claude model, and for mock 4 the owner filled 20 marks without reading the questions.
4. Step 6: 108 findings and 4 rewrites. The 57 D4 findings are ignored, and the page gives no reason.
5. "2 bad marks of 209": no person gave a bad mark in round 1, yet a second review found 7 problems of mock 1 as a set.

## Missing (for the owner)

- A comparison with the real exam (difficulty, style). "The owner passed" is the only link.
- A check of the answer keys by someone other than a model, for mocks 4 and 5.
- An estimate of the error rate that remains, and a way to report an error.
- The list of known limits of each mock, for the candidate.
- A warning that 10 ideas appear in all 5 mocks, and that the letters of mocks 1 to 4 depend on the portal shuffling the options.
- The total cost (about 86 dollars for the 5 mocks), which the reader must add up.

## Claims stronger than their evidence (for the owner)

- "So the mocks are more than practice. They are a working example of the exam's principles in action." The evidence supports "the build used these principles", not that the questions are good. This is in the paragraph that the owner wrote.
- "Here a reader marks the questions, to show if those checks are right": for 2 of 5 mocks the reader was a model.
- The guide's claim that a reviewer with no earlier reasoning catches more, cited as the reason for call 4a, while call 4a caught nothing.
- "84 of the 95 ideas are in 2 to 4 mocks", shown as a good spread, while 10 ideas are in all 5.
- "This tab was built the same way … This matches the multi-pass review of the guide": no count of what those agents found on this tab.

## Result of the assistant

No fix. Each item asks for new text, new evidence or a choice about how the tab presents its limits.
